Relativistic Black Hole Simulation · Kerr Metric

Real-time ray-marched visualization of a rotating (Kerr) black hole with an accretion disk. Every pixel on screen is computed by tracing a photon backwards from the camera through curved spacetime.

1. Spacetime Geometry — Kerr Metric

The simulation uses the Kerr metric — the exact solution of Einstein's field equations describing a rotating, uncharged black hole. The two key parameters are:

  • Spin a* — dimensionless angular momentum, 0 = Schwarzschild (non-rotating), 0.998 = near-maximal (Thorne limit)
  • Rs — Schwarzschild radius (event horizon scale), set to 1.0 in our units

Event Horizon

The outer horizon is computed as:

r₊ = M + √(M² − a²) = 1 + √(1 − a*²)

ISCO — Innermost Stable Circular Orbit

The inner edge of the accretion disk is set to the ISCO — the closest orbit where matter can stably rotate. Uses the Bardeen–Press–Teukolsky formula:

Z₁ = 1 + (1−a²)1/3 · [(1+a)1/3 + (1−a)1/3]
Z₂ = √(3a² + Z₁²)
r_ISCO = 3 + Z₂ − √((3−Z₁)(3+Z₁+2Z₂))

For a non-rotating hole → r_ISCO = 6·M. For a* = 0.998 → r_ISCO ≈ 1.24·M.

2. Photon Geodesics — RK4 Integration

Light rays are bent by gravity. Instead of drawing straight lines, each ray is integrated through curved spacetime using the geodesic equation in the orbital plane:

d²u/dφ² = (3/2)·Rs·u² − u    where u = 1/r

This is solved with a 4th-order Runge–Kutta integrator (RK4). Each step:

  • Takes 4 slope estimates k₁…k₄
  • Combines them as (k₁ + 2k₂ + 2k₃ + k₄)/6
  • Error scales as O(h⁵) — very accurate

Adaptive step size: smaller near the horizon and inside the disk, larger in empty space. This gives sharp shadows and smooth lensing.

3. Accretion Disk — Shakura–Sunyaev Model

The disk is a volumetric emissive medium between r_ISCO and r_outer = 14·Rs. Its temperature profile follows the classic thin-disk model:

T(r) ∝ r−3/4 · (1 − √(r_in/r))1/4

Peaks at r ≈ 1.36·r_in, drops towards both edges. The peak temperature is ~35,000 K → white-blue color at the inner edge, cooling to red at the outer edge.

Density Structure

  • Vertical Gaussian: exp(−y²/h²), thickness h ∝ r
  • Radial smoothstep window between r_in and r_outer
  • Two superimposed spiral arms: sin(angle·k + phase)
  • Anisotropic noise stretched along the orbital direction
  • Differential rotation: Ω(r) ∝ r−3/2 (Kepler)

4. Relativistic Effects

Doppler Beaming

Matter on the approaching side of the disk moves towards the camera at relativistic speed. Its radiation is blue-shifted and dramatically brightened. The formula:

D = 1 / [γ·(1 − β·cosθ)]

where γ = 1/√(1−β²) is the Lorentz factor, β = v/c, and θ is the angle between velocity and line of sight.

Gravitational Redshift

Light escaping from deep inside the gravity well loses energy:

g_grav = √(1 − Rs/r)

Combined Intensity

The observed intensity uses the relativistic transfer law:

I_obs = g³ · μ_e · I_emit    where g = D · g_grav

Planck Color Temperature

Emission color is derived from the observed black-body temperature T_obs = T_emit · g using a piecewise approximation of Planck's law (Tanner Helland). This naturally produces the blue/gold/red gradient.

5. Photon Ring

The bright thin ring at the shadow's edge is formed by photons that orbited the black hole 1–2 times before escaping to the camera. Our simulation detects them by tracking the minimum distance of each ray to the horizon. Rays that grazed close but escaped (captured = 0, minDist ≈ 0) form the ring. Rays that were captured (captured = 1) form the shadow.

The ring is asymmetric: brighter on the prograde side (moving towards us), dimmer on the retrograde side — matching real EHT observations of M87*.
